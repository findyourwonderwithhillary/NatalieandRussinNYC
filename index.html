A Note Before You Begin

October is the month New York shows off. The light goes gold in the late afternoon, the sidewalks are busy without being unbearable, and the whole city seems to be walking somewhere with purpose. You have three nights and a Monday holiday, which is exactly enough time to feel like you live there for a minute.

All three of the hotels below sit in the same pocket of Manhattan, roughly between Madison Square Park and the Empire State Building. It is a neighborhood where you can walk out the door at seven in the evening and have a genuinely great dinner within four blocks, in about six different cuisines, without ever getting on a train. Each hotel offers a different version of the same weekend. Read them side by side and tell me which one sounds like you.

First, about the number

You gave me a target of $1,000 for the three nights, and I want to be straightforward with you rather than send this over and let you discover it in the totals. Every one of these comes in higher. That is not because I ignored the budget. It is because of what this particular weekend costs in this particular city.

October is the most expensive month of the year for New York hotels, and your dates land on the heaviest weekend in it: a three-day holiday alongside a convention at the Javits Center that draws roughly a quarter of a million people. The city's short-term rental rules also mean there is no apartment option to fall back on. What that adds up to is a floor of about $450 a night for a well-located, well-run hotel that you will actually enjoy walking back to.

I could get you closer to $1,000, but the savings would come out of the parts of the trip you told me matter. It would mean a further-out neighborhood, a longer commute to dinner, or a property I would not personally put you in. That trade does not seem worth roughly a hundred dollars a night to me, though it is entirely your call and I am glad to go pull those options if you want to see them.

So think of the range below as the honest market for this weekend. All three are good. The spread between them is small enough that you should choose on character rather than on price.

Where You'll Be

NoMad, Flatiron, and the blocks between

The Empire State Building anchors the top of this map at Fifth Avenue and 34th. Madison Square Park sits at the bottom, with the Flatiron Building at its southern tip. In between are three distinct dinner neighborhoods: Koreatown along 32nd Street, which stays open long past midnight; Curry Hill on Lexington in the upper twenties; and the restaurant blocks around Madison Square Park, which hold some of the best dining rooms in the city. Everything marked here is within a twenty minute walk of all three hotels.

The Options

Three hotels, three versions of the weekend

The Freehand New York

23 Lexington Avenue at East 23rd Street · Gramercy

The Freehand occupies a 1928 building on the corner of Lexington and 23rd, and it has more personality than anything else on this list. The interiors lean warm and collected rather than sleek, with vintage furniture, textiles, and a good deal of commissioned artwork, and the whole place feels designed by someone with a point of view rather than a brand standard. The Broken Shaker on the roof is one of the better hotel bars in the city and stays lively enough that you may not make it out for a nightcap somewhere else.

The Premium Queen is the room to have here. It sits above the entry-level category with more square footage and better light, which matters in a building of this age. The location puts you at the top of Gramercy with Curry Hill immediately north, Madison Square Park three blocks west, and the 6 train a few steps from the door.

This is the pick if you want the hotel itself to be part of the trip rather than a place to sleep between meals.

Arlo NoMad

11 East 31st Street, between Fifth and Madison · NoMad

Arlo is the closest of the three to the Empire State Building, four minutes on foot, and the rooftop bar on the 31st floor puts the building directly in front of you at eye level. It is the best view of the three by a wide margin, and it is free to you as a guest.

The tradeoff is the room. The City Queen is small by any standard, in the neighborhood of 150 square feet, with a queen bed, built-in storage under and around it, and a floor-to-ceiling window that does most of the work. Arlo designs deliberately this way, spending the square footage on common areas instead, so the lobby, the library, the courtyard, and the roof are where you actually spend your waking time. If you are the kind of travelers who drop your bags and leave, this trades beautifully. If either of you needs room to spread out in the evening, it will feel tight by the third night.

Location is the other argument in its favor. You are one block from Koreatown, two from the Morgan Library, and a short walk from Herald Square, which puts nearly every subway line in the system within reach.

Moxy NYC Chelsea

105 West 28th Street, between Sixth and Seventh · Chelsea at the Flower District

The Moxy Chelsea is the only one of the three offering a king bed at this price, and the City View category means you are facing out rather than into a light well. Rooms here are also compact, though noticeably less so than the Arlo, and the design is sharper and more contemporary: dark tones, brass, good lighting.

The building sits in the middle of the Flower District, and the hotel leans into it. The lobby is a glass conservatory full of plants, reached by a garden elevator, and the ground floor holds an Italian restaurant and a cafe that is genuinely good for morning coffee. Upstairs is the Fleur Room, a glass-walled bar on the 35th floor with velvet, marble, and a view that runs from the gold roof of the New York Life Building all the way down to the harbor. It is the most striking room of any hotel on this list.

The block itself is quieter than the other two addresses, more wholesale storefronts than restaurants, which cuts both ways. Dinner requires a five to ten minute walk rather than a two minute one, but you are also closer to Chelsea, the High Line, and the gallery district than either of the others.

Eating and Wandering

What is within walking distance

None of this requires a reservation months out, though the marquee dining rooms are worth booking once you have chosen dates and a hotel. I am happy to handle those.

Dinner Worth Planning Around

Coté on West 22nd is a Korean steakhouse with a Michelin star, and the dry-aged cuts are the reason to go. Gramercy Tavern on East 20th remains one of the warmest dining rooms in New York and is easier to get into at the bar. Both are worth reserving in advance.

Koreatown, After Dark

West 32nd Street between Fifth and Broadway is stacked three floors high with barbecue, noodle shops, karaoke, and dessert cafes, and much of it runs past two in the morning. This is the single best late-night eating block in Manhattan and it is four minutes from the Arlo.

Curry Hill

Lexington Avenue in the upper twenties has been the city's South Indian corridor for decades. Saravanaa Bhavan for dosas, Kailash Parbat for chaat, and a row of spice shops in between. Inexpensive, excellent, and directly outside the Freehand.

Morning

Eataly at Fifth and 23rd for coffee, pastry, and the best grocery browsing in the neighborhood. The Union Square Greenmarket runs Monday, Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday, six blocks south, and Saturday is the big one.

A Drink

The Flatiron Room on West 26th pours several hundred whiskeys with live jazz most nights, and it is the most atmospheric room in the neighborhood. The Broken Shaker at the Freehand is the other answer, and it is open to you whether or not you are staying there.

Culture and Views

The Morgan Library on Madison at 36th is a small and extraordinary museum in J.P. Morgan's own study and library, and it is rarely crowded. For height, the Empire State Building observatories are two blocks from the Arlo, and Summit One Vanderbilt at Grand Central is the more dramatic modern alternative.

An Afternoon West

The High Line begins near 30th Street and runs south through Chelsea to the Meatpacking District, ending at Chelsea Market and the Whitney. It is about a fifteen minute walk west from any of the three hotels and makes an easy Sunday.

Just Outside the Door

The Flower District on West 28th sets up before dawn and is worth seeing early, when the sidewalk fills with palms and cut stems. Madison Square Park has the original Shake Shack and rotating public art. Rizzoli on Broadway at 26th is one of the last great bookstores in the city.

Getting In

From LaGuardia to the neighborhood

LaGuardia is the closest of the three airports to Manhattan and, since the terminal rebuild, one of the better ones to land in. A taxi or rideshare into this part of Midtown runs roughly 25 to 40 minutes outside of rush hour, and closer to an hour if you arrive between four and seven in the evening on a weekday. Expect somewhere in the range of $55 to $85 including tolls and tip, with rideshare surging higher at peak times. The yellow cab stand is well marked at each terminal and the fare structure is predictable.

If you prefer transit, the Q70 LaGuardia Link bus is free and runs from all terminals to the Jackson Heights subway station, where the E train takes you into Midtown in about twenty minutes. It is genuinely fast, and with only carry-on bags it is a reasonable choice. With checked luggage on a holiday weekend, take the car.

Once you are here, you will not need a car again. Everything in this proposal is walkable, and the subway covers anything that is not.

Whichever you choose

You are going to walk out of the lobby on Saturday evening into a neighborhood that smells like the flower market and roasting chestnuts, and you are going to eat something excellent within four blocks.
